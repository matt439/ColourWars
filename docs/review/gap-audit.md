# ColourWars — gap audit

> A review for **missing and inadequate functionality**, not a bug hunt. Run
> 2026-08-11 against the tree at that date, by 26 agents: nine parallel finders,
> one per functional dimension; an independent skeptic per dimension, prompted to
> refute every claim rather than agree with it; a completeness critic that named
> three unexamined areas, with follow-up finders for each; and one synthesis pass
> that merged duplicates across dimensions and ranked what survived.
>
> **88 findings survived challenge** — 67 confirmed, 20 partial, 1 unverified,
> none refuted outright. Two of the partials are the author's own corrections,
> made after the run and recorded in place at 1.1 and 2.1.
>
> **This document is the audit as written, and is not updated as gaps are
> closed.**

## Scope

Two yardsticks, applied together: what a small local-multiplayer game ships with,
and what this repository owes Labrador as its first and real client. The nine
dimensions swept were persistence, UI and menus, input, engine-surface coverage,
the match loop, presentation and feel, content and data, robustness and
lifecycle, and learnability; the critic added colour identity, build identity and
glyph coverage.

Findings are split three ways. **Class 1** is the game as a game. **Class 2** is
its role as the engine's demonstration — engine surface the one real client never
exercises, and things a reader cannot learn here. **Class 3** is engine-api
friction: cases where the client wanted something ordinary and the API forced a
bad answer. Those are reports to file against Labrador, not work to do here —
per CLAUDE.md, engine changes belong in the engine repository.

## Verification notes

Read the confidence markings; they are not decoration.

- Every finding was challenged by an agent instructed to search for an existing
  implementation the finder had missed, to check the cited lines said what was
  claimed, and to default to refuted when a claim was vague or unreproducible.
  18 claims came back **partial** — the capability exists but is thinner than
  claimed, or a load-bearing part of the argument failed — and are demoted to
  the lower-confidence section with the reason stated. The absence of any
  outright refutation is worth reading as a fact about the skeptics as well as
  about the findings.
- **The skeptics could only check the code, not the intent.** Two findings —
  including the one the audit led on — were withdrawn in part by the author
  after the run, both because a deliberate borrowing from Splatoon read to the
  finders as an omission. Nothing in the tree records either decision, so no
  amount of adversarial verification would have caught them. Treat any finding
  of the form "the game does not do X" as reported against genre expectation,
  and check it against intent before acting.
- **L12 received no verdict at all** and rests on one agent's reading. It is
  marked unverified in place.
- Each finder was capped at eight findings for its dimension, and most
  dimensions returned eight. Coverage is therefore a floor, not a ceiling.
- Four things were ruled out of scope by construction and are listed, with their
  reasons, under "Considered and deliberately not flagged" at the end: the
  absence of tests here, the silent wave bank, Windows-only, and the OOP design
  itself. No finding proposes replacing a hierarchy with values; every suggested
  shape adds to the hierarchies.

## Executive summary

The engine-facing craft here is high and the game-facing surface is thin. Team, score and player identity are carried by hue and nothing else, and the pair that carries them is rolled per match with no accessibility route around it and no non-colour channel behind it — one of the three pairs is pink against green (`game/objects/team_colour.cpp:27-42`). The options screen is four rows — resolution, fullscreen, apply, back (`game/states/main_menu.cpp:506-514`) — with no volume, no rebinding, no accessibility and no route in from a paused match. Three of the four advertised game modes are wired to an error beep (`game/states/main_menu.cpp:713-720`). Nothing tells a player which button shoots, anywhere in the game or the repository.

**What I would do first, in order:** (1) a fixed-palette / high-contrast option, because the accessibility hazard is live on one roll in three and the fix is small; (2) a controls page plus per-screen button legends, the cheapest completeness win in the list; (3) fix the destruction-order UB on quit-during-match (`external/labrador/engine/app/application.h:81` vs `game/states/game_states.cpp:115-118`) — it fires on the most common exit path; (4) per-player paint attribution, so the end-of-match reveal has more to reveal than which of two bars is longer; (5) file the engine-friction items in Labrador, of which the missing audio bus and the missing action map are the two the engine has explicitly been waiting for a client to ask for.

**Two findings were withdrawn in part after review**, both where the audit had read a deliberate borrowing from Splatoon as an omission. The match shows no running score because the count is withheld to make the ending a reveal (1.1), and each match's palette is drawn at random from a small authored set of pairs because that is how the model assigns ink (2.1). Neither is challenged; what survives of each is narrower and is recorded in place — per-player attribution to give the reveal something to reveal, and a route around the roll for a player who cannot read one of the pairs.

Read the rest knowing the finders judged against general expectations of the genre and could not see design intent that is not written down anywhere in the tree. That is a limit of the audit, and also a finding: it is the case for D7.

Where the repo is already good, it is genuinely good: the save's atomic write path, the JSON error messages, the pause menu's lost-pad ownership fallback, and the level-object builder's throw-with-the-key-named discipline are all better than most shipped code. None of that is manufactured severity below.

# Class 1 — Gaps in the game as a game

## Theme 1. The match: one mode, nothing above a single round

The most consequential cluster. 1.1's headline claim is withdrawn on the author's
correction; everything else here is confirmed.

### 1.1 The withheld score is deliberate; what survives is per-player attribution
**impact: medium · effort: medium · status: partial** *(headline claim withdrawn — see below)*

*Withdrawn:* the finding as filed was that the match shows no running score, so the game's premise is invisible while it is played. **That is a design decision, not a gap.** Coverage is withheld until the clock expires so the result lands as a reveal — the model is Splatoon, which likewise shows no coverage percentage during a match and makes the count itself the payoff. A live percentage would spend the ending to buy mid-match tension, which is the opposite of the trade this game has chosen. Nothing below asks for one, and the once-only rescan at `game/objects/level.cpp:530-559` is exactly as often as the design needs it.

*What survives:* per-player attribution. `PaintTile` records a `PlayerTeam` and never which player painted it (`game/objects/paint_tile.h:81`), and `grep -rniE "\bkill|deaths|frag"` over `game/` returns nothing — so the reveal can only ever be two team bars and "Team A Wins!" (`game/objects/level_end_info.cpp:63-79`). Four players who just spent four minutes painting learn nothing about what any of them did.

*Why it matters:* withholding the score for four minutes makes the reveal the whole payoff, which makes it the one screen worth investing in rather than the one to leave thin. Per-player contribution is the material a reveal is built from, and it is what 6.2's record board and any future MVP or stat line would read.

*Shape:* `WeaponBuilder` already receives `setting.num` (`game/objects/player_builder.cpp:83-91`) — carry it on the projectile and record it on the tile beside the team. `LevelEndInfo` then gains a per-player breakdown that `ResultsMenuPage` can reveal after the team result; it already delays, fills two bars over time and reveals widgets in sequence (`results_menu.cpp:39-86`), so the theatre is built and only wants more to show. *(A `PaintLedger` updated incrementally in `PaintTile::paint`, which already knows the displaced team and the tile's area, would make the end-of-match count O(tiles flipped) rather than O(all tiles) — worth having if the attribution walk grows, not worth doing on its own.)*

### 1.2 Mode Select advertises four modes and delivers one
**impact: high · effort: large · status: confirmed**

*Today:* five rows — Standard, Team Deathmatch, Deathmatch, Practice, Back (`game/states/main_menu.cpp:644-692`). Standard transitions; the other three are `focus_.add(tdm_.get(), [this]{ play_wave(error_sound_); })` under the comment "Three modes that are not built yet" (`main_menu.cpp:713-720`) — and they take `menu_style().unhighlight`, the same colour as the working Back row, so nothing on screen marks them unavailable. `grep -rniE "GameMode|game_mode|MatchMode"` over `game/` returns zero hits; `MenuLevelSettings` has exactly four members and no mode (`game/states/menu_level_settings.h:31-36`); `Level`'s only end condition is `timer_ <= 0.0f` (`game/objects/level.cpp:192-216`), and `LevelState` is a presentation sequence (countdown/active/zoom_out/overview/finished), not a rules seam.

*Why it matters:* a stranger's second interaction with the game is a buzz. And "paint area at time-up" is the only question the codebase can answer about a finished match.

*Shape:* a `MatchRules` base — `virtual bool is_over(const Level&) const`, `virtual LevelEndInfo result(const Level&) const`, `virtual std::wstring headline(const LevelEndInfo&) const` — with `RulesPaintTimed`, `RulesTeamDeathmatch`, `RulesDeathmatch`, `RulesPractice`, and `RulesPaintSuddenDeath : RulesPaintTimed`. `MenuLevelSettings` grows a `GameMode`; `LevelBuilder::build_level` constructs the rules beside the scene; `Level::update_state` asks `rules_->is_over(*this)`. This is the deepest OOP modelling opportunity in the whole list and the one that would prove `Scene`'s four phases support more than the flow they were extracted from.

### 1.3 The one-player path starts a match against nobody and announces a 100% victory
**impact: high · effort: large · status: confirmed**

*Today:* the 1-player row is fully wired — count 1, team A, one-player layout, the connected pad's slot, skipping team select (`game/states/main_menu.cpp:862-877`). `PlayerBuilder` builds exactly one `Player`; `Level::apply_player_inputs` drives players by pad slot only. `grep -rniE "\b(bot|ai|cpu|npc|opponent|dummy)\b"` over `game/` returns **nothing at all**, and `brain|pathfind|difficulty` likewise. `LevelEndInfo` returns `team_b_area` 0, the bar fills to 100% and the screen says "Team A Wins!" (`game/objects/level_end_info.cpp:5-17, 63-79`). Practice, the natural home for a solo mode, is one of the three beep rows.

*Why it matters:* solo is the most likely first session, and the only way to learn the controls before friends arrive — which matters doubly given 1.5 below.

*Shape:* a `PlayerController` base with `virtual PlayerInputData poll(const Level&, const Player&) const`; `PadController` wraps today's slot lookup, `BotController` derives further into `BotPainter` / `BotDuellist`. `Level::apply_player_inputs` asks each player's controller instead of indexing the pad vector — a one-line change once `Player` holds one. This is also the cheapest possible demonstration that the engine's input/simulation split is real rather than assumed.

### 1.4 No match structure: one fixed 240 seconds, no rounds, no tie-break
**impact: medium · effort: medium · status: confirmed**

`"duration": 240.0` (`game/content/tuning.json:3`) is copied into `Level::timer_` (`game/objects/level.cpp:36`) and can only be changed by editing tuning and rebuilding. `grep -rniE "\bround\b|best.?of|sudden.?death|overtime|tiebreak"` over `game/` finds only prose. A draw is reachable and terminal (`level_end_info.cpp:5-17, 76`). The end menu's five rows never record that you have now played twice (`game/states/game_states.cpp:277-288`). *Shape:* `duration`/`rounds` on `MenuLevelSettings`, a `MainMenuMatchOptions` page using the existing left/right value-row machinery, and a `MatchSeries` owned by `GameMenu` (which already outlives every match) so `ResultsMenuInitial` can print "Round 2 of 3 — Team A leads 1-0".

### 1.5 Four players can all confirm the same team; the match then runs 4v0
**impact: medium · effort: small · status: confirmed**

The only gate on a confirm is `team != PlayerTeam::none` (`game/states/main_menu.cpp:954-959`); `all_players_confirmed()` counts confirmation states and never teams (`main_menu.cpp:1015-1025`); `set_level_settings` writes them through unchecked (`main_menu.cpp:999-1014`). All three levels list 4 spawns per team, so `PlayerBuilder` never trips. `grep -rniE "handicap|auto.?balance"` returns nothing. The page already owns an `error_sound_` (`main_menu.h:60`) whose only three uses are the dead mode rows. *Shape:* a `TeamRule` consulted immediately before the transition at `main_menu.cpp:989` — refuse, beep, unhide a "Both teams need a player" widget (the hide/unhide idiom already exists in stage select). Add a "Random teams" row; put handicaps on `MenuPlayerSettings` as a per-player paint multiplier.

## Theme 2. Colour is the only channel, and there is no route around it

This game's score, team identity and player identity are carried by hue and by nothing else. 2.1's mechanism claim is withdrawn on the author's correction; the rest is confirmed.

### 2.1 The discrete random pair is the design; the missing piece is a route around it
**impact: high · effort: medium · status: partial** *(mechanism claim withdrawn — see below)*

*Withdrawn:* that each match's palette is a hand-authored pair drawn at random from a small closed set is a design decision, not a gap — the model is Splatoon again, which likewise assigns each match an ink pair from an authored set rather than generating one or letting players pick freely. Neither the roll, the three-pair list, nor `rand() % 3` is challenged below, and nothing here asks for a colour picker.

*What survives:* there is no accessibility route around the roll, and no sight of it before it is spent. `TeamColourTools::team_colours(TeamColourPair)` — the only API that could constrain or select a pair — **has zero callers anywhere** (`game/objects/team_colour.cpp:44-56`); the roll at `game/objects/level_builder.cpp:41` is the sole path. `MenuLevelSettings` has no colour field, `SaveData` has no colour field, and `grep -i "colou?r.?blind|deuteran|protan|tritan|high.?contrast"` over `game/` returns two incidental comments and nothing functional.

*Why it matters:* `PINK_GREEN` is (255,0,127) against (0,255,0) — a red-green confusion pair; `BLUE_YELLOW` sits on the tritan axis. The roll is uniform, invisible before the match and unrepeatable, so a player with a deficiency loses roughly one match in three to a palette nobody chose, and the only escape is pause → Restart, which re-rolls blindly at the same odds and costs the match. Splatoon ships colour-blind ink options for exactly this reason, and the discrete-pair model is what makes them cheap: the fix is a filter on which pairs the roll may draw, not new art and not a change to how a pair is picked.

*Shape:* promote `TeamColourTools` into a policy hierarchy — a `TeamColourSource` base with `virtual TeamColour choose() const`, and `RandomTeamColourSource` (today's behaviour, unchanged and still the default), `SafePairTeamColourSource` (the same roll over the subset that survives a deficiency filter), `FixedTeamColourSource(TeamColourPair)`. `LevelBuilder` already holds it by `unique_ptr` (`level_builder.h:32`), so line 41 becomes `team_colour_->choose()`. Add the setting to `SaveData` and one Accessibility row to the options page from 3.2. Showing the drawn pair on the team-select page (see L6) costs nothing and removes the "what am I about to get" half without touching the roll at all.

### 2.2 The team palette is the only screen-visible colour left in C++ — the structural blocker under 2.1
**impact: high · effort: small · status: confirmed**

Every other on-screen colour is data: presentation.json carries menu, heading, box, health and timer colours through a `read_colour` helper that accepts a CSS name or hex and throws with the key named (`game/presentation.cpp:44-62`), and level files carry per-object colours ("SADDLE_BROWN", `king_of_the_hill.json:97-207`). The team palette is in neither file — `grep -i "team|colour"` over `tuning.json` finds only debug colours, the paint block and per-team *sound* arrays (`tuning.json:92-95`, proof that per-team keying is idiomatic here). So every change to the pair list is a code edit and a release.

This is the finding that matters most in this theme, because the design 2.1 records **wants more pairs**: the model runs to somewhere under ten and this ships three, and `team_colour.h:39-40` already anticipates growth — "A new pair goes above this line". Adding the fourth should be an edit to a content file that an art pass can make, and today it is a rebuild. The same block is what a deficiency filter would tag.

*Shape:* a `team_palette` block in presentation.json (named colours plus named pairs) parsed with the existing `read_colour` into a struct hanging off `Presentation`; `LevelBuilder` already receives the `Presentation*`. Keep the pairing enum in C++ — the code switches on it — but let the numbers and the pair list be content, and size the roll from the list rather than from the enum.

### 2.3 No non-colour team channel anywhere
**impact: high · effort: large · status: confirmed**

`PaintTile::draw` draws one frame and one splash tinted by team (`game/objects/paint_tile.cpp:44-54`), and both are single keys — `"frame": "pixel"`, `"splash": "splash_1"` (`tuning.json:25-32`) — so both teams paint identical pixels. A player is one animated body in `burlywood` plus one shared `uniform_texture` tinted by team (`player.cpp:36-41, 96-122`, `tuning.json:62-70`). Weapons and projectiles each store one team `Colour`. The two results bars name two frames that resolve to the **identical 32×32 source rect** (`sprite_sheet_1.json:157-181`); team A fills from the left and B from the right, an unlabelled convention, and the only text is "Team A Wins!" which never says which bar was A. Art for a second channel is already in the sheet and referenced by nothing: `splatter_20/40/60/80` and `proj_roller` (`sprite_sheet_1.json:417-468`), `player_1_right`/`player_2_right` (`:586-611`). *Shape:* widen `TeamColour` (or wrap it in a `TeamStyle`) with a per-team frame name and splash strip so every existing `const TeamColour&` parameter keeps its shape; feed it from a `team_a`/`team_b` block in tuning.json's `paint` section, mirroring the loop-sound shape. On the results screen, overlay each fill with a team mark and prefix the percentages "A"/"B".

### 2.4 Nothing in a match says "this is you"
**impact: high · effort: small · status: confirmed**

`draw_gameplay_interface` takes no identity argument and has no fifth draw helper (`interface_gameplay.h:23-48`). `Level::draw_player_overlay` draws dividers, HUD, optional debug and countdown — nothing over any player (`level.cpp:444-508`) — and the end-of-match overview draws the whole arena with **no overlay at all** (`level.cpp:436-441`). The debug block lists rect/velocity/camera/input and no player number. Teammates with the same weapon are pixel-identical draws. The only cue that your character is yours is that the camera is centred on it. Meanwhile the game spells identity everywhere else: "Pause Menu P1"…"P4" (`pause_menu.cpp:47-62`), "Player N" on team select (`main_menu.cpp:1133`). Compounding: the one team-tinted HUD element is the ammo bar, and on a `PINK_GREEN` roll team B's ammo bar is (0,255,0) directly adjacent to a health bar in (0,128,0) — same hue, same shape, same size. *Shape:* a `PlayerMarker` drawable (a `GameObject`/`TextureObject` subclass like `PaintTileSplash`) owned by `Level` and drawn in the world camera above each player's `center()` — `Level` already switches cameras mid-list at `level.cpp:458-469`, and the overview branch can pass the same callback instead of none. Plus a "P1" label beside the ammo bar from a new `hud.player_number` block.

## Theme 3. The options screen and what a player can set

### 3.1 There is no volume control of any kind
**impact: high · effort: medium · status: confirmed** *(merged from four independent findings)*

*Today:* every volume in the game is an authored constant read from `tuning.json` (`:15-16, 34, 57-60, 89, 109, 123, 141, 161`), `presentation.json` (`:80, 133-134`) or the level file's `music_volume`, passed to one of **74** `play_wave`/`play_effect` call sites across 10 files. `SaveData` has no audio field. The options page has four rows and none is audio. The runtime half of the audio API is entirely unused: `set_effect_volume`, `set_effect_pitch`, `set_effect_pan`, `effect_state`, `is_effect_looping` (`sound_bank.h:78-82`) have zero call sites in `game/`. Pausing hard-pauses every voice (`level.cpp:132-137`) because ducking is not expressible.

*Why it matters:* "turn the music down" is the most universally expected settings row in any game, and on the machine that does have the wave bank the menu music loops on every page at a fixed 0.03. This is also the loudest unmade engine complaint in the repo — see friction item F1.

*Shape (client-side, buildable today):* `master`/`music`/`effects` floats on the save; three rows on `MainMenuOptions` using the existing `adjust_focused_value` left/right machinery; scale at the emitters. Note the game objects have no `Save` reach at all — `grep "GameData|Save\*"` over `game/objects/` returns zero hits — so a gain reaching `Player`/`Weapon`/`Level` needs either new constructor parameters through every builder or the engine-side bus in F1. That constraint is itself the finding worth filing.

### 3.2 The only options screen is display-only, and unreachable from a match
**impact: high · effort: medium · status: confirmed**

Four focusable rows (`main_menu.cpp:506-514`), reachable only from `MainMenuHome` (`:262-267`) — I traced `GameMenu::open_menu`/`build_page` (`game_states.cpp:38-71`) and there is no other route. `PauseMenuInitial` offers Resume/Restart/Quit (`pause_menu.cpp:210-228`) and `EndMenuInitial` five rows, neither with an options entry, so **nothing is adjustable once a match starts**. Missing beyond audio: gameplay settings (match length and respawn delay are already data — `tuning.json:3, 53`), accessibility (theme 2), "restore defaults", any pending-vs-applied marker, and any revert-on-timeout after a resolution change. *Shape:* section the page (Display / Audio / Match / Accessibility), add an `OptionsPage` subclass as a fourth pause-menu row pushed above the pause page so the match stays suspended, and push a `ConfirmRevert` countdown page after a resolution change.

### 3.3 Nothing teaches the controls — no controls screen, no legend, no credits, one button prompt in the whole shell
**impact: high · effort: medium · status: confirmed** *(merged from three findings)*

`grep -rniE "controls|how to play|tutorial|help|credits"` over `game/` (`.cpp`, `.h`, `.json`) returns **zero player-facing hits**; `MainMenuScreen` has eight enumerators and none is a help screen (`main_menu.h:13-23`); the repo has no docs directory and README documents building, not playing. Of the 66 wide literals in `game/states/*.cpp` exactly one names a control: `L"Press A to proceed"` (`results_menu.cpp:186`). Team select, weapon select and stage select each respond only to left/right and say so nowhere. Weapon descriptions in presentation.json describe behaviour and name no trigger. The scheme is not guessable: shoot is the right trigger past a threshold, jump is A *or* the left trigger, aim is the right stick with a radial deadzone, drop-through is the left stick past 0.8, pause is Start on release, Back toggles a debug overlay (`player_input.cpp:31-62`). *Shape:* `MainMenuControls` and `MainMenuCredits` beside the existing seven pages (the repo already carries a NOTICE naming third-party work that the running game acknowledges nowhere), plus a shared `PromptBar` on `MainMenuPage` that every page fills with its own legend. Put the strings in presentation.json beside `weapon_descriptions`, which proves the file can hold user-visible prose. An optional legend over the three-second `start_countdown` (`level.cpp:159-191`) reaches every player once per match for free.

**Related:** neither README nor CLAUDE.md states that the game is gamepad-only (`grep -rni "controller|gamepad|xinput"` over both → one hit, an architecture note). A reader who builds correctly with no pad plugged in gets a title card that ignores every key, with the only in-game Exit one page further in. One README line, and one `UiTextDropShadow` on `MainMenuTitle` that reads "Connect a controller" when `connected_slots()` is empty. **impact: medium · effort: small · confirmed.**

## Theme 4. Controllers: identity, hot-plug, bindings, feel

### 4.1 No join or pad-claiming flow, and a mid-match disconnect is silent
**impact: high · effort: medium · status: confirmed** *(merged from four findings)*

*Menu half.* `connected_slots()`/`connected_inputs()` walk the live pad list and compact away empties, and are recomputed **inside `update()` every frame** on every per-player page (`main_menu.cpp:36-61, 930, 1207, 1537`) and again at commit, where the fallback is `slots[i] : i` (`:1012`). `MenuPlayerSettings::num` defaults −1 and is the whole of the binding. `MainMenuPlayerCount` offers 1–4 with no reference to how many pads exist (`:862-889`); nothing anywhere compares `player_count()` to the pad count. Three reachable consequences: 4 players with 2 pads makes team select **unfinishable** (drive loop is `i < num_inputs && i < player_count` at `:934`, but `all_players_confirmed()` requires all four); a page escaped past that welds characters to empty slots; and a pad plugged into slot 1 mid-page shifts every later player onto someone else's row. `grep -rniE "\bjoin\b|ready.?up|claim|lobby|roster|seat"` over `game/` returns two unrelated hits.

*Match half.* An absent slot's `PlayerInputData` is left default-constructed and handed to the player (`player_input.cpp:70-81`, `level.cpp:319-335`); `GameLevel::update` checks only pause and finished (`game_states.cpp:186-208`). `just_disconnected` — declared at `gamepads.h:61` — **has zero callers in `game/`**. A flat battery leaves a motionless body soaking paint for the rest of the round with nothing on screen, and a pad returning in a different slot silently drives a different character, which `game/objects/player_input_data.h:17-26` states outright as a decision "this game has not made yet". The engine's `gamepads.h:20-26` says in as many words that binding a player to a slot "is a decision a game makes" and that it reports the edges "so that it can make it, and makes none itself."

*Already solved next door:* `PauseMenuPage::pausing_player_input` falls back to any pad still in the match when the pausing pad vanishes, with a paragraph explaining why (`pause_menu.cpp:74-111`). The hard part is done; the trigger and the page are missing.

*Shape:* a `PadRoster` owned by `GameMenu` beside `MenuLevelSettings`, holding `PadSeat { int slot; bool present; }` filled in join order and never re-derived; a `MainMenuJoin : MainMenuPage` between mode select and player count where an unclaimed pad presses A to take a seat; `player_count()` reads the roster; `set_level_settings` copies `roster.seat(i).slot`. Give it `player_of_slot(int)` so `pause_menu.cpp:47` says P2 when it means P2. In the match, a `PadWatch` on `GameLevel` reading `just_disconnected(slot)` for the slots the match owns pushes a `PauseMenuDisconnected : PauseMenuPage` — the family already suspends the match and ducks the mix, and already lets a non-owning pad answer.

### 4.2 No rebinding, no controls data, no action concept — and the engine says it is waiting on exactly this
**impact: high · effort: large · status: confirmed**

Bindings are literals at two independent sites: `PlayerInput::player_input` (`player_input.cpp:31-65`) and `menu_input_consts::action_buttons` (`menu_input.h:31-38`). Across the whole game only 8 of 14 `GamepadButton` enumerators are ever named; x, y, both shoulders and both stick-clicks appear nowhere. No bindings file, no per-player bindings, no persistence. `external/labrador/engine/input/gamepad.h:9-16` names the absence *and names this client as its cause*: "an action map … the paint-shooter and the sample both spell their bindings in code, and neither has a rebinding screen to fill such a table from." `ARCHITECTURE.md:124-127` repeats it and `PHILOSOPHY.md:313` puts "which actions exist, and their bindings" on the game's side of the seam. *Shape (client-side first, which is the order the engine asked for):* a `GameAction` enum and a `Bindings` class holding a `BindingSource` hierarchy — `ButtonBinding`, `TriggerBinding` (owning its own threshold, the thing `gamepad.h:38-45` says the caller must own), `StickAxisBinding` — each answering `held/pressed/released` against a `GamepadState` pair. `PlayerInput` and `MenuInput` take a `const Bindings*` and the two tables become one. Persist beside the save; add `MainMenuControls` with a per-action "press a button" capture state, reusing `MenuInput::armed_` so the opening press is not captured.

### 4.3 Input feel is frozen in headers, against the repo's own rule
**impact: medium · effort: small · status: confirmed**

`trigger_shoot_threshold` 0.6, `trigger_jump_threshold` 0.6, `stick_deadzone` 0.5 are `constexpr` in `game/objects/player_input.h:9-16`; the menu hysteresis pair 0.8/0.8 in `menu_input.h:16-17`. Meanwhile `drop_down_analog_threshold` — the same kind of number on the same stick — is `tuning.json:49`. `GameTuning` has no input section at all. So `player.cpp:411` compares the *raw* stick y against a tuned value while `player_input.cpp:51` deadzones the same stick's x with a header constant, and a feel pass costs a rebuild of most of `game/objects/` (`player_input.h` is included by `level.h`). One deadzone value serves both the walk axis and the aim vector, two uses `gamepad.h:57-65` says want different deadzones. No sensitivity, no invert-Y (aim is an absolute stick angle, so an inverted player has no recourse at all). *Shape:* an `InputTuning` struct on `GameTuning` read from an `"input"` block; `PlayerInput`/`MenuInput` take a `const GameTuning*` as every other object does. Per-player preferences (invert, sensitivity) belong on the save and on the `MainMenuControls` page from 4.2. *(The two menu thresholds are shell feel — `presentation.json` may be their proper home given this repo's split.)*

### 4.4 Menu steps land on the release, with no auto-repeat and no arming across a transition
**impact: medium · effort: small · status: confirmed**

`MenuInput::menu_input`'s direction block emits only on the *return* into the absence band — `was.y < -0.8 && stick.y > -0.8` and its siblings, and `no_dpad_now && before.is_down(button)` (`menu_input.cpp:87-129`). The hysteresis is deliberate and documented ("a held stick is one step and not a stream of them", `menu_input.h:14-18`); the *timing* is not. Three consequences: every menu move lags to the let-go; there is no repeat delay or interval anywhere (`grep repeat|hold_timer|initial_delay` over `game/states/` and `engine/ui/` → nothing), so walking a stage list is one full push-and-release per item; and the `armed_` flag the header argues at length is essential covers only the three action buttons, not directions — so holding left while confirming the last team pick cycles that player's weapon on the next page, which is exactly the scenario the comment describes. *Shape:* a per-slot `DirectionState { Direction held; float time_held; bool armed; }` beside `armed_`; arm and emit on the crossing *into* the band using the same `!just_connected && went_down` shape the buttons use, then repeat after an initial delay. Both numbers join the `InputTuning` block above. Every page keeps calling `focus_.move(0, input.direction)` untouched.

### 4.5 Jump is same-frame-only: no input buffer, no coyote time
**impact: low · effort: small · status: confirmed**

`do_jump` launches only where `is_on_ground() && jump_pressed` both hold on one frame (`player.cpp:520-526`); `is_on_ground()` is a straight test of four grounded states with no recently-grounded timer, and `player.cpp:224` sets `in_air` the frame ground is lost. `jump_pressed` is a one-frame edge with no persistence. At the fixed 60 Hz step both windows are inside ordinary human timing error. The variable-height half is done properly and fully tuned from data (`tuning.h:123-125`), which is what makes the omission read as an omission. *Shape:* `jump_buffer_timer_` and `coyote_timer_` beside `air_time_`, both zeroed on launch, with the two windows joining `PlayerTuning` beside `jump_max_time`.

## Theme 5. Feedback, feel and presentation

### 5.1 Every screen change is a hard cut, and the level build freezes on the last menu frame
**impact: medium · effort: medium · status: confirmed** *(merged from three findings)*

`GameLevel::build_and_enter_level` destroys and rebuilds inline, and says so: "Building a level is a blocking call worth many frames" (`game_states.cpp:159-184`). The whole path from stage-select's pop through push → enter → init → build runs inside one pending-op drain, so **no frame is drawn** between the stage-select page's last one and the match's first. The build generates every paint tile for every paintable face (`structure_paintable.cpp:133`, against walls the code elsewhere calls "fifteen hundred" tiles). At startup, `ShowWindow` fires inside `initialize()` (`application.cpp:152`) while `load_manifest` is not called until `main.cpp:103`, so the client area sits on the `COLOR_WINDOW` brush for the whole asset walk. `grep -rniE "fade|crossfade|loading|spinner|progress"` over `game/` and `engine/` returns **zero hits in both trees** — so no engine helper was missed. Audio matches: the menu track is hard-stopped entering a match (`main_menu.cpp:1570`), the level track starts on the countdown tick (`level.cpp:166-171`) with the build sitting in the silence between, `Level::suspend` hard-pauses rather than ducks, and the results and end screens have no track at all (`end_menu.cpp:8-16` resolves three waves and no effect).

*Why it matters:* a freeze with no indicator is indistinguishable from a hang, at the most exciting moment in the flow, on the most-travelled path in the game. The capability is present and the choice was not made — the results screen already delays, fills two bars over time and reveals widgets in sequence (`results_menu.cpp:39-86`).

*Shape:* (1) a `FadeOverlay` `UiTexture` owned by `MenuPage` plus `begin_exit(next)`, so every existing page inherits it without touching its `init()`; (2) a `LoadingPage : MenuPage` pushed before `build_and_enter_level()` — it draws the chosen stage's icon and name on its first draw and runs the build on its second update, converting the freeze into a frame the player sees, and giving a future thread-pool build somewhere to draw a progress bar; (3) a `MusicDirector` on `GameMenu` ramping `set_effect_volume` for `play(track, fade_in)` / `stop(fade_out)` / `duck(amount)` — client-side, since `set_effect_volume` exists.

### 5.2 The in-match camera never sets a scale — field of view is whatever the pane's pixel size is
**impact: high · effort: medium · status: confirmed**

`CameraTools::calculate_camera` copies `prev_camera` and touches only `translation` on every branch, and builds its bounds rectangle straight from `viewport_size` (`camera_tools.cpp:26-90`); `Camera::scale` is never written on the follow path. `Level` calls this as the only in-match camera source (`level.cpp:84-90, 342-348`), so the scale stays 1.0 for the whole match — the game's only use of `Camera::frame` is the end-of-match pull-back. Panes are raw halves and quarters of the back buffer; supported resolutions run 1280×720 to 3840×2160; `close_quarters` has camera bounds 6100×5350. So a player at 4K sees **nine times the world area** a player at 720p sees, in the same arena with the same weapon ranges, and four-player 720p gives each player a 640×360 window — less than one screen-width of the sprayer's 1100 u/s projectile flight. The dead-zone border is a pixel floor of 400/250 clamped to half the pane, so the border tuning changes meaning between a fullscreen and a quarter pane. There is also no lead/lookahead and no shake hook.

The sharpest part: **this codebase already knows how to do this one directory away.** `MenuPage::ui_camera` derives its scale from a 1920×1080 `DESIGN_RESOLUTION` (`menu_page.cpp:142-159`), so the menus are resolution-independent and the match is not.

*Shape:* a `LevelCamera` per player owned by `Level` beside the `Player*` it keeps, holding scale, shake offset and lead, wrapping `CameraTools`. Derive the scale once per pane from a designed world extent in tuning's `match` block via `Camera::frame`. The dead zone then needs expressing in world units — see friction F5.

### 5.3 Hits on the world burst; hits on a player do not
**impact: high · effort: large · status: partial** *(demoted framing; the specific absences below are confirmed)*

*What is real:* `Projectile::on_contact` sets `for_deletion(true)` and nothing else (`projectile.cpp:99-108`). `Player::draw` returns early while dead, so the body simply disappears (`player.cpp:96-101`); `respawn` teleports and plays a wave (`:659-668`); damage subtracts health and plays a throttled wave with **no tint, flinch or interruption** (`:300-317`) — `player.cpp` has no `set_colour`/`set_hidden`/alpha call at all, and `PlayerAnimationState` is idle/walk/run/jump/fall only, so there is no hurt clip. No muzzle flash, no trails, no screen shake (`grep -i "shake|particle|hit_stop"` over `game/` and `engine/` → nothing). All five projectile kinds share the single strip `projectile_3` (`tuning.json:179, 197, 209, 221, 239`), so a sniper jet, a mist particle and a thrown bucket are the same two-frame sprite at different sizes. Shipped art nothing references: `splatter_20/40/60/80`, `proj_roller`.

*Why this is "partial":* the original framing ("nothing in a fight has a visual reaction") is wrong. Every projectile reaching paintable geometry fires a play-once `PaintTileSplash` tinted with the scoring team's colour at the contact point (`paint_tile.cpp:26-31, 64-72`) plus a splash wave (`structure_paintable.cpp:121-128`). The confirmed gap is narrower and still substantial: **player-directed and screen-level feedback**.

*Shape:* a `LevelEffect : artattack::AnimationObject + artattack::GameObject` family whose `for_deletion()` returns `is_paused()` once a non-looping strip lands on its last frame, with `EffectBurst`, `EffectDeath`, `EffectRespawn`, `EffectMuzzleFlash` beneath it. Spawn from the phase-3 slot the engine leaves to the game — between `scene_->resolve()` and `scene_->end_tick()`, where `update_players_after_resolve` already runs — by walking `scene_->contacts()` (see D4). Damage flash needs no new object: `Player::draw` already passes a tint per draw call, so a `damage_flash_timer_` lerping it is a pure read.

### 5.4 HUD text scales by three different rules, and the clock is raw seconds
**impact: medium · effort: small · status: confirmed**

`draw_timer` computes `scale = resolution.x / look.timer_reference_width` and scales the glyphs and shadow offset (`interface_gameplay.cpp:154, 161-166`); `draw_respawn_timer` twenty lines below passes literal `1.0f` and an unscaled shadow offset for a 144pt face (`:188-193`); the countdown is built once at a fixed `countdown_scale` of 2.0 and only re-centred (`level.cpp:97-107, 124-130`). `HudPresentation`'s own comment concedes the split. The `resolution` passed in is the *pane*, not the screen, so at four-player 1080p the clock draws at scale 0.25 while the respawn digit and "GO!" stay at fixed pixels — the split-screen layout shifts the disagreement as much as the display does. The clock prints `ceil(seconds)` as a bare integer against a 240-second match: "240", "239", "238". *Shape:* one `HudPresentation::reference_width`, one pane scale computed at the top of `draw_gameplay_interface` and passed to every helper; give the countdown a reference width too; a `format_clock(float)` helper for mm:ss, with a tuning flag for a last-ten-seconds highlight — the cheapest end-of-match tension the HUD can buy.

## Theme 6. What survives a launch

### 6.1 Nothing about the match a group just played survives the process
**impact: medium · effort: medium · status: confirmed**

`MenuLevelSettings` carries the whole setup — player count, stage, screen layout, per-player team/weapon/pad slot (`menu_level_settings.h:9-36`) — and `GameMenu` holds it for the session, deliberately, so "change teams" returns to a live page (`game_states.cpp:73-107`). Then it is discarded at exit: `grep -rn "menu_level_settings_"` returns four hits, none of them a file. Four people re-walk mode → count → team (per-player confirm) → weapon (per-player confirm) → stage on every launch to reach last night's configuration. *Shape:* a `LastMatchSettings` save section written from `GameMenu::start_match()`, with `GameMenu::init()` seeding from it and each select page pre-positioning its cursor — the pages already resolve "where is this value in the list" (`slot_of`, `cycle_weapons`). Guard staleness the way stage select already does: an unknown stage name falls back to the first (`main_menu.h:266-269`), a player count above `connected_slots()` falls back to it.

### 6.2 Every match result is computed, shown once and discarded
**impact: medium · effort: medium · status: confirmed**

`LevelEndInfo` is passed by value into `ResultsMenuPage`, animated, and destroyed with the page (`results_menu.h:13-14, 34`). `grep -rniE "high_score|leaderboard|profile|unlock|statistic|\bstats\b"` over `game/` returns **zero hits**. For a party game whose score is a single comparable percentage per stage, a record board is the cheapest retention feature there is — "can we beat 68%?" is the reason to play the same stage twice. *Shape:* a `MatchRecords` save section keyed by stage name (the manifest name `MenuLevelSettings::stage()` already carries), holding best winning percentage, matches played and a team tally. `GameLevel::open_results_menu()` is the one place a completed result exists. `ResultsMenuInitial` gains a "NEW BEST" line; `MainMenuStageSelect` shows the stage's best under its icon using the existing `slot_display_name` helpers.

**Enabling refactor (see also lower-confidence L1):** both of the above want a save that can hold something structured. A `SaveSection` base — `virtual const char* key()`, `virtual void read(const JsonValue&)`, `virtual void write(SaveWriter&) const` — with `Save` owning a `vector<unique_ptr<SaveSection>>` and catching per section, turns `VideoSettings`, `AudioSettings`, `LastMatchSettings` and `MatchRecords` each into one self-contained subclass. It adds no capability on its own; it is worth doing as the vehicle for 3.1, 6.1 and 6.2, not ahead of them.

## Theme 7. Failure paths, lifecycle, and there is no build you can hand anyone

### 7.1 Nothing pauses when the window loses focus — the clock runs down with neutral input
**impact: high · effort: medium · status: confirmed** *(engine friction, see F4)*

`WM_ACTIVATEAPP` → `Application::on_activated`/`on_deactivated` suspend/resume the pad reader and nothing else (`application.cpp:300-314, 526-538`); nothing reaches the state stack. `State::on_suspend`/`on_resume` mean "something was pushed above me" and are only called from `apply_push`/`apply_pop`. `run()` ticks whenever the queue is empty, minimised or not. A suspended reader answers disconnected for every slot, `PlayerInput` turns that into neutral input for all four, and `Level::update` decrements the clock every tick. So alt-tabbing mid-match runs the match without its players, at full music volume, into whatever the user switched to. `grep -i "focus|activate|minimi|background"` over `game/` finds only `FocusGroup` widget focus. The machinery is already here — `GameLevel::on_suspend`/`on_resume` duck the whole mix correctly (`game_states.cpp:217-229`). Only the wire from the shell to the stack is missing.

### 7.2 The state stack is destroyed after every service it borrows — quit-during-match is UB
**impact: high · effort: small · status: confirmed** *(engine friction, see F3; correctness item, not only a gap)*

`Application` derives from `StateContext` (`application.h:81`) and declares every service as a derived member (`:154-170`); `~Application` never touches the stack and `~StateContext` is `= default`. C++ destroys derived members first and the base subobject last, so `frames_` — and every `State` in it — dies **after** every service. And this game's architecture is built on exactly those destructors: `~GameLevel` calls `viewport_manager()->set_layout(previous_layout_)` (`game_states.cpp:115-118`) and `~Level` calls `sound_bank_->stop_effect(music_)` plus `stop_player_sounds()` (`level.cpp:66-71`). Closing the window during a match (X button, Alt+F4 → WM_DESTROY → PostQuitMessage → `run()` returns → app destroyed at `main.cpp:122`) calls `set_layout` on a destroyed `ViewportManager` and `stop_effect` on a `SoundBank` whose owner is gone. The engine's own round-2 review recorded this ordering and judged it *latent* on the explicit ground that every state destructor was then implicit or defaulted — that premise has since expired, and the finding was never implemented. CLAUDE.md advertises both of those destructors as the architectural win of the state stack.

### 7.3 One exception handler, labelled "startup failure"
**impact: high · effort: medium · status: confirmed**

`wWinMain` wraps load, initialize, manifest walk and `app.run()` in one `catch(const std::exception&)` printing "startup failure: …" to stderr and raising `MessageBoxA(..., "ArtAttack - startup failure", ...)` (`main.cpp:28, 124-132`). `Level::update_level_logic` throws "Player out of bounds" from inside a live match (`level.cpp:310-316`); `PlayerBuilder` throws when a stage has too few spawns. Both arrive as a box that says *startup failure*, during play, after which the process exits and the match is gone. `grep "SetUnhandledExceptionFilter|MiniDump|catch (...)"` over the whole repo → one hit, engine-side. No log file, no per-phase framing, no crash record, and one throw site cannot be caught at all: `tick()` runs from inside `window_proc`'s WM_PAINT while the user drags the window. The asset/content error path underneath is genuinely excellent by contrast — `read_json_file` and `JsonValue` name the file, position and key. *Shape:* `class GameError : std::runtime_error` with `ContentError` (fatal, names the file) and `MatchError` (recoverable, carries stage and player count); a `FatalReport` writing `./crash_log.txt` and showing a phase-appropriate dialog; `wWinMain` gains a phase enum and a `catch(...)`; `GameLevel` catches `MatchError` and pops back to the menu with a new `EndMenuAction::failed`.

### 7.4 Layout never reconciled with the actual window
**impact: high · effort: medium · status: confirmed** *(engine friction, see F5)*

Every viewport, camera and menu computes from a four-value 16:9 `ResolutionManager` enum that snaps anything else to 720p and cannot represent 3440×1440. The window is created with the resolution as the **outer** size with no `AdjustWindowRect` anywhere in the engine, so the client area is ~16×39 px smaller than the layout from the first frame. `Renderer::window_size_changed` "returns whether anything was rebuilt, which is the signal the shell wants for re-run the layout" (`renderer.h:249-251`) — and `Application::on_window_size_changed` `std::ignore`s it. Fullscreen is a restyle to `WS_POPUP` + `SW_SHOWMAXIMIZED`, so the back buffer follows the desktop while the enum does not; the client's own comment at `main_menu.cpp:358-369` describes the corner-drawing symptom, works around it by refusing to apply a resolution while fullscreen, and writes down the missing API verbatim: *"Nothing on ResolutionManager or Application reports the desktop size."* `grep "renderer()"` over `game/` → zero hits, so the game never asks. The client-side half of this is available today: call `application()->renderer()->back_buffer_size()` when applying fullscreen.

### 7.5 No DPI awareness
**impact: medium · effort: small · status: confirmed**

`game/settings.manifest` is 16 lines of `supportedOS` GUIDs with no `dpiAware`/`dpiAwareness` — and it declares `xmlns:asmv3` on line 1 and never uses it, the fingerprint of a cut-down template. Nothing in CMake adds one; the engine never calls `SetProcessDpiAwarenessContext`, `GetDpiForWindow` or `AdjustWindowRectExForDpi`, and `window_proc` has no `WM_DPICHANGED` case. So on the 125%/150% scaling that is default on most current Windows laptops, a 1280×720 window is composited to physical by DWM with bitmap filtering — exactly the blur a game that insists on `TextureFilter::point` everywhere is avoiding — and 3840×2160 on a scaled 4K display asks for a window larger than the screen. **One catch the manifest edit does not cover:** `game/pch.h:9-10` pins `_WIN32_WINNT` to 0x0601, which hides `SetProcessDpiAwarenessContext` from every game TU, so the manifest declaration is necessary but not sufficient. The engine's own round-2 review recorded the same finding and it is unactioned.

### 7.6 No shippable artefact, and nothing the exe can say about which build it is
**impact: high · effort: medium · status: confirmed** *(two merged findings)*

`CMakePresets.json:10` declares `installDir` for every preset and **nothing fills it** — `grep "install(|CPack|export("` over `CMakeLists.txt`, `game/CMakeLists.txt` and `cmake/` returns zero hits, and no generated `cmake_install.cmake` contains a single `file(INSTALL`, so `cmake --install` copies nothing. `.github/workflows/` holds only `ci.yml`, with no artifact upload and no release job; `git tag` is empty. There is no documented distribution act at all — README stops at "runs from anywhere". Meanwhile `out/build/x64-debug/game/` contains `.lib`, `.exp`, `.ilk`, `.pdb`, `CMakeFiles/` beside the exe, and the built `sound_bank_1.xwb` (154 MB on this checkout) that NOTICE says is not ColourWars' to redistribute — so any naive packaging must first make a licence decision.

On identity: `project(ColourWars LANGUAGES CXX RC)` enables RC and `game/resource.rc` is 104 bytes holding one `ICON` line, so the built exe's `VersionInfo` is empty on every field (verified against the binary). The icon is the stock DirectX sample icon. `grep -i version` over `game/**` returns three irrelevant hits. The pinned Labrador revision — the authoritative answer to what engine this exe contains — is nowhere in the binary either. A bug report reads "the paint game crashed" and cannot be mapped to a commit.

*Shape:* hoist the staging enumeration at `game/CMakeLists.txt:146-173` into one list consumed twice (the existing `copy_if_different` and matching `install()` rules) so the dev tree and the package can never disagree; `install(TARGETS)` + `$<TARGET_RUNTIME_DLLS:...>`; `set(CPACK_GENERATOR ZIP)`; a release workflow on tag push. Give `project()` a `VERSION`, `configure_file` a `game/build_info.h.in` filling `constexpr` strings for the version, `git describe`, the submodule SHA and the configure timestamp — a header of constants beside `directory_consts`, which respects the six-pointer `GameData` invariant. Drive `VERSIONINFO` from the same numbers, and surface the stamp on `MainMenuTitle` (laid out from a `version_stamp` presentation entry) and in both message-box texts. Those two captions still read "ArtAttack" while the window and title screen read "Colour Wars" (`main.cpp:129`, `save.cpp:172` vs `main.cpp:46`, `main_menu.cpp:107`) — a `DISPLAY_NAME` constant in the same generated header fixes that in passing. *(CLAUDE.md grandfathers old **identifiers**; a MessageBox caption is not one.)*

### 7.7 The only diagnostic is a match-only overlay whose FPS is a constant by construction
**impact: high · effort: medium · status: partial** *(the framing is demoted; three of four sub-claims confirmed)*

`DebugText` prints `dt` and `1.0f/dt` from `Level::frame_dt_`, which is `StepTimer`'s **fixed** elapsed time under `SetFixedTimeStep(true)` — so it is exactly 0.016667 and 60.0 on every machine, in every frame, whatever the game is doing. `GetFramesPerSecond()` does move but `timer_` is private and only `reset_elapsed_time()` is exposed. The overlay is toggled by a `Player` member, so the menus, the load, the results screen and the whole audio subsystem have no readout, and there is no object count, draw-time, stack-depth or "the sound bank is the silent substitute" indicator. No log file, no `OutputDebugString` wrapper, no build stamp. *Why demoted:* the accompanying claim that the game's messages are invisible to a player is false — `save.cpp:169-173` and `main.cpp:128-130` each pair stderr with a `MessageBoxA`, and `save.cpp:163-168` explains that reasoning explicitly. The gap is observability, not user messaging. *Shape:* a `DiagnosticsOverlay` sibling to `DebugText` owned by `GameMenu` rather than `Level`, so it is available on every screen, fed by a new `Application::frames_per_second()` (a two-line engine addition — see F6).

## Theme 8. Content pipeline and the C++/JSON line

### 8.1 A level file is checked key-by-key at first use, so a broken stage kills a started session
**impact: high · effort: medium · status: confirmed**

`LevelLoadedInfo` keeps the parsed document and reads each key on demand — thirteen accessors, each a fresh walk (`level_loaded_info.cpp:13-80`). The `register_kind` callback touches exactly two of them, `display_name()` and `icon_frame()` (`main.cpp:91-101`). The other **eleven** — four bounds rectangles, two spawn arrays, both object arrays, the sound block — are first read by `LevelBuilder::build_level` inside `GameLevel::init()`, i.e. after the player picked a stage and pressed A. There is no `validate()` anywhere in `game/`, and nothing resolves the sheet/frame names a level quotes against sheets the manifest has already loaded. `Application::run` has no try/catch, so the throw lands in `wWinMain`'s handler and pops "ArtAttack - startup failure" — a whole four-player session lost, with the wrong phase named. `main_menu.cpp:1714-1718` records that this has already bitten once (`stage_test_1`, "a frame the sheet has not carried for three stages"). This directly contradicts CLAUDE.md's own rule that a content bug "must fail at startup, not three screens later". *Shape:* either build a decoded `LevelDefinition` in the constructor, or add `void validate(const RenderResources&) const` that touches every accessor and resolves every frame, throwing a `LevelContentError` naming the stage — called from `main.cpp`'s `register_kind` callback (manifest order already loads sheets before levels). Assert both teams list at least `Gamepads::max_count` spawns, and that every spawn is inside `out_of_bounds`.

### 8.2 Content numbers are type-checked and never range-checked
**impact: high · effort: small · status: confirmed**

Every read goes through `JsonValue`, so type and presence fail loudly. Beyond that the only value checks in the whole content path are five enum-name lookups, one array-length check, and the colour helpers. No numeric read is bounded and no two fields are ever compared. Live consequences: `match.zoom_out` may be 0, producing 0/0 — `Level::zoom_out_camera_ratio` guards it at runtime and its own comment says *"The proper home for this is a range check where the tuning is read"* (`level.cpp:253-268`); `paint.size` may be 0 and is divided by twice with the result cast to int (`structure_paintable.cpp:140-148`); and `tuning.json:123` **ships `"volume": 3.0`**, which `SoundBank::clamp_levels` silently turns into 1.0. The whole argument for 350 numbers in JSON is that a balance pass can edit them without a rebuild; that pays only if a wrong number comes back as a sentence naming the key. *Shape:* a `ContentValue` wrapping `JsonValue` with `positive_number`, `number_in(lo, hi)`, `unit_number`, `rectangle_inside(outer)`, each throwing with `where()`; plus cross-field assertions at the end of each constructor. *(Drop the suggested `countdown < duration` check — they are independent sequential timers.)*

### 8.3 The level format is documented only by the loader, and the file shaped like a template would throw
**impact: high · effort: small · status: confirmed** *(two merged findings)*

A stage is 476–961 hand-written lines of absolute coordinates. tuning and presentation are documented in headers plus `_comment` arrays; the level format has nothing — the object vocabulary exists only as if-chains at `level_object_builder.cpp:40-108, 121-236`. There is no docs directory, no schema, no version key in any of the four JSON files. All four files under `levels/unused/` use `"level_name"` where the loader requires `display_name` **and** `icon_frame`, and `test_1.json` names two collision types the loader rejects — so a reader who copies the file literally named `base.json` gets a startup failure, which reads as "the build is broken" rather than "that file is old". Undocumented besides: which `collision_type` each `type` may claim, that `rectangle_tlbr` is an accepted alternate spelling, that a `StructurePaintable` needs `paintable_faces{}`, that spawn arrays must be at least as long as the team, and that the 111 per-object `"name"` keys the shipped levels write are read by nothing. The three `.pub` files beside the levels (110–120 KB Microsoft Publisher documents, one misspelt `turebulence.pub`) are plainly the source the coordinates were traced from and are mentioned in no tracked file. *Shape:* `LEVEL_FORMAT.md` keyed by the loader with a `type` × `collision_type` table; a `level.schema.json`; a corrected `template.json` that actually loads; delete or port `levels/unused/`; add `"format": 1` to the four files.

### 8.4 Player-visible text is C++ literals, including two that read "ERROR" on screen
**impact: medium · effort: medium · status: confirmed**

72 wide literals across the shell (main_menu 45, pause_menu 13, end_menu 6, results_menu 2, level 1, level_end_info 5). ### 8.7 Text has no defined behaviour for a character the font cannot draw
**impact: high · effort: medium · status: confirmed**

The input half accepts arbitrary Unicode on purpose — `widen()` is a correct `CP_UTF8` conversion and `presentation.h:147-148` says so. The output half accepts **95 characters**: I re-parsed the `DXTKfont` header of every `.spritefont` in the tree (33 distinct, including the engine sample's) and without exception each holds 95 glyphs, contiguous, 0x20–0x7E — MakeSpriteFont's default region, which nothing chose and nothing records. A miss is a **throw**, not a skip: DirectXTK's `FindGlyph` falls back to `defaultGlyph` and throws `"Character not in font"` when it is null, and nothing in engine, samples or game ever calls `SetDefaultCharacter`. Both engine text entry points walk glyphs, and `TextObject::remeasure()` calls the measuring one from the constructor and from `set_text` — so the throw usually lands at *construction*. `application.cpp` has no try/catch, so it reaches `main.cpp:124-131` as a modal captioned **"ArtAttack - startup failure"**. Concretely: a curly apostrophe in `weapon_descriptions.sprayer` survives startup and kills the process four screens later when weapon-select builds its `UiTextDropShadow`; an accented stage name does the same. This is the one content value read and never checked, in a file whose loader otherwise names the file, position and key on every path. *Shape:* a `TextSanitiser` at the two `widen()` boundaries transliterating the punctuation authors actually produce and mapping anything else outside 32–126 to `?`; plus a load-time `ContentTextCheck` walking every content string against the font `Presentation` names for it, throwing "presentation.json: weapon_descriptions.sprayer contains U+2019, which gill_sans_mt_bold_24 has no glyph for". Doing the second properly needs friction item F7.

**Related, confirmed:** the fonts' `cmd.txt` (75 bytes, `MakeSpriteFont "Courier New" myfile.spritefont /FontSize:32 /FontStyle:Bold`) describes **none** of the seven shipped atlases (Gill Sans MT Bold 24/36/48/72/144, Gill Sans Ultra Bold 144, Courier New Bold 16) and passes no `/CharacterRegion`. Nothing in the build touches fonts. The contrast with audio is stark: for waves the repo ships the definition, the tool *and* `cmake/build_wave_bank.cmake`, documented in README and NOTICE. So fonts are the one asset class that cannot be regenerated, and the 95-glyph ceiling is effectively permanent. Mirror the wave-bank recipe: `fonts.json` naming family/size/style/regions, `cmake/build_sprite_fonts.cmake` with the same build-it-when-you-can shape, and an explicit `/CharacterRegion:0x20-0x7E` today so the current range is a stated decision.

**Also confirmed:** there is no text layout at all — no wrapping, no fitting, no truncation. Line breaks are literal `\n` typed into `presentation.json` by eye, and `weapon_description` has an x offset and no width. `text_bounds()` exists, is measured eagerly and cached for exactly the right reasons, and is used for layout **once** in the entire game (`level.cpp:125-131`, centring the countdown). Any string longer than the author's runs off the panel with nothing to detect it. A `WrappedText : UiTextDropShadow` given a max width — re-wrapping exactly when `remeasure()` already runs — plus a width in presentation.json, would let the author state the box and the code state the wrap, which is precisely the rule CLAUDE.md sets out.

# Considered and deliberately not flagged

Assessed and ruled out of scope, so their absence from the report is a decision rather than an oversight:

- **"There are no tests in this repository."** Deliberate and documented; the tests live with the engine behind `PROJECT_IS_TOP_LEVEL`. A `ctest` run from here is empty, not broken. Not reported in any phrasing.
- **The silent wave bank on a fresh clone.** Deliberate, documented in NOTICE and CLAUDE.md, with the recipe shipped and the loader substituting a silent bank plus one stderr line. Note that the volume finding (3.1 / F1) is *not* this: it is about a settings row and a saved gain, and is equally missing with the bank present. The one place the bank does touch this report is 7.6, where any packaging step must decide what to do with the 154 MB `.xwb` NOTICE says is not ColourWars' to redistribute — that is a licence decision the packaging work inherits, not a complaint about the bank.
- **Windows-only, Direct3D 11 + XAudio2.** Inherited from the engine, not a gap. The absence of any *keyboard or mouse path* is reported (3.3, F-adjacent) as device coverage — a padless first launch cannot dismiss the title screen — which is a different question from the platform choice.
- **The deep inheritance and virtual dispatch.** This is the design, and every suggested shape above adds to the hierarchies rather than flattening them: `MatchRules`, `PlayerController`, `TeamColourSource`, `SaveSection`, `MenuRow`→`MenuValueRow`→`MenuSliderRow`, `PerPlayerMenuPage`, `LevelEffect`, `BindingSource`, `StructureRotated`/`StructureMoving`, `PauseMenuDisconnected`. No finding here says "replace the hierarchy with values", "this should be a variant" or "too much virtual dispatch".
- **Old names in identifiers** — `ArtAttackGame.exe`, `artattack::`, `ArtAttackWindowClass`, the `artattack_content` target. CLAUDE.md grandfathers these explicitly and they are fine. Only the two user-visible message-box *captions* are raised, and only as a rider.
- **The two disjoint git histories** and the `original-2022` branch. Deliberate and documented.
- **`game/save.cpp` vendoring rapidjson.** Not filed as a client defect — the engine's `ARCHITECTURE.md` records the boundary as a settled decision. Only the re-derivable durability protocol is raised, as F9, and at low priority.
