    Why does this world fight over paint?

    Splatoon's premise sounds mad, but it works because the story explains the mechanic: ink is what squids are made of, and Turf War is their sport. Five writers pitched 30 premises from five angles: strange species, the world as a drawing, mundane life pushed to extremes, myth and apocalypse, and childhood. An editor merged them into 23. Three judges scored each one on how well the story explains the game, how memorable it is and how well it fits the art, and whether one developer can carry it with art and jokes. The top six are drawn below in the Paint.exe recipe.

    All three judges proposed the same hybrid: Save Changes with Feed the Hues

    The universe is untitled.bmp, a 16-colour file open since 1998 with one palette slot left. The two team colours are petty, hungry minor gods campaigning for that slot, and the box-heads are the only people the Hand can draw: their congregations. When the Hand clicks Save, the colour covering more of the canvas gets the slot and the loser dithers to grey. That Save is your end-of-match reveal, and the next match is a rematch because the program forgets its palette when it closes. The greys in the world are failed gods, which is why the world has no colour. The first commandment is THOU SHALT NOT DRAW WELL.

    If you want to keep the painter tradesmen: combine The Last Coat with Save Changes. The decorators are contracted to paint the next universe, and the Big Bang is the Hand clicking Save: UNIVERSE #4,118,002: MOSTLY ORANGE.

    What separated the top premises from the rest

    • They make the crude art true inside the world. Aliasing is physics, drawing badly is piety, and the box head is a house.
    • They explain why the world is grey. A full palette, rationed colour, extinct colour or starving gods turn the "team colours are taken" art rule into story.
    • They explain the rematch. The program forgets, a new universe begins, or the season comes round again.
    • The random colour pairs were the weakest link almost everywhere. Save Changes explains them best: the Edit Colours dialog only offers candidates in pairs.
    • Pure tradesman premises were safe but not weird enough. The tradesman versions that scored made the trade cosmic: The Last Coat, and the Hueherds of Hue Season.

    Ten ways to push Paint.exe

    You picked Paint.exe Maximalism. Each variation pushes it one way, and every one applies the round-1 fixes: heavier figures, a keyline under the paint, and all three colour pairs tested. The same three judges ranked them.

    The figure

    The box-head was the weakest part of round 1 in split screen. Eight versions, at real size and at the 28 px a quarter pane gives them. All three judges picked E.

    Recommended: figure E, made slightly heavier

    • Overalls: a team-coloured bib and trouser legs on a white shirt. It's the largest block of colour for the least ink, and it's still your box-head.
    • Line weight: about 7–8 px at 110 px tall, drawn outside the fill so the outline never eats into the team colour.
    • Teammate marker: a bobble or antenna on top of the head, about twice the size in the study. Only shapes above the head survive at 28 px; stripes and flaps vanish.
    • Face: two square eyes and one flat mouth, no nose. X eyes and a splat of the enemy colour when hit.
    • Tools: a back-tank sprayer whose gauge window shows ammo, and a roller drawn as a cylinder. Neither crosses the torso.
    • Key art: keep the fat-limbed figure F for posters, the store capsule and the title screen.

    The combined recipe

    Working separately, the three judges converged on one style. Zoom 400% sets the value system, Tool Props supplies the terrain, Airbrush supplies the shadows, and True Maximalism is reserved for the title screen.

    Canvas and line

    A white Paint canvas with a visible canvas edge. Chunky aliased mouse-wobble lines, with the staircase visible. Treat it as a texture, not a literal low-res grid, so the camera can still move smoothly. Leave one or two white pinholes in every bucket fill.

    Three value tiers

    The backdrop is light-grey doodles only. Walkable ground is mid-to-dark grey with a black outline. Only players and paint are saturated. The rule a player learns without being told: a black outline means you can stand on it.

    Team colours are taken

    No blue, orange, pink, green or yellow anywhere in the world, the sun included. The jokes say so: SUN (YELLOW IS TAKEN). Non-team palette chips such as maroon, teal and brown are allowed on small props.

    Paint

    A flat bucket fill on a 2.5–3 px black keyline, baked into the stamps. Roller trails are flat with short drips; sprayer coats are patchy with long drips. Mist in the air has no rim and gains one when it lands.

    Terrain from Paint's tools

    Eraser blocks, a marching-ants selection box, a text box that types platform|, a resize-handle step, and a pencil ramp as a one-off landmark. Drop the palette-strip floor and the ghosted "Untitled".

    Depth with the airbrush

    Square-speck airbrush shadows under every floating platform (they replace the hang-lines), and slabs darkening toward the underside. Baked into tiles, never a full-screen haze. The sprayer's mist is the same speck in team colour.

    Where the maximalism goes

    Density goes in the top band and at the screen edges; the play lanes stay calm. Per screen: 3–5 big grey gags, 2–3 captioned Paint jokes, and one by matt (age 7) signature per stage. Full-volume WordArt, rainbows and multiple suns appear only on the title screen, capsule and trailer.

    Arena themes

    One rule set with swaps: the default white page, a Tools stage, a Black Canvas night stage (the same tiers inverted), and a kid's-landscape stage whose walkable ground keeps its own darker fill.

    Acceptance check

    Every new stage or surface passes two checks. First, a swatch grid of all three colour pairs on each surface. Second, the 28 px figure line-up at quarter-pane size.

    What the panel said

    Three judges ranked every concept, each through a different lens. The scores on the cards are the average of their three.

    Rules that held across every style

    • Put a dark value under the paint. Dark ledges or a dark keyline below the paint was the strongest predictor of readability in a quarter pane.
    • Keep team hues out of the world. Blue ballpoint ink and navy riso ink swallow the blue team. Use charcoal or graphite for world ink.
    • Outline the paint. Yellow disappears on cream and white grounds without a dark rim, which affects most of these concepts.
    • Make figures heavier. Hairline stick limbs turn into specks at 480×270. Use thicker lines, a filled body, or a larger cap and tool.
    • Prototype paper grain and halftone in motion. Full-screen texture reads as static in a small pane and may shimmer when the camera scrolls.
